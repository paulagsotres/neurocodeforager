summary:
Keypoint-MoSeq is an unsupervised behavior-segmentation algorithm that fits a switching linear dynamical system to keypoint tracking data (from SLEAP, DeepLabCut, etc.), simultaneously denoising keypoint jitter and identifying discrete behavioral "syllables," outperforming prior clustering methods (B-SOiD, VAME, MotionMapper) at capturing true behavioral transitions across mice, rats and flies.

abstract_long:
Traditional MoSeq requires 3D depth video and fails when applied directly to 2D/3D keypoint data, because high-frequency tracking jitter gets mistaken for behavioral transitions. The authors rebuilt MoSeq as a switching linear dynamical system (keypoint-MoSeq) with three hierarchical levels — a discrete syllable sequence, a low-dimensional pose trajectory, and per-keypoint noise estimates — so the model can attribute an implausible single-keypoint jump to noise while preserving a smooth underlying pose trajectory. Validated against accelerometry, striatal dopamine photometry, and expert behavior labels, keypoint-MoSeq produces syllables (median duration ~400 ms) that align far better with true behavioral transitions than B-SOiD, VAME, or MotionMapper, and generalizes across species (mice, rats, fruit flies) and timescales (from ~35 ms sniff-aligned head movements to multi-second syllables). Note: the code is free for academic and non-commercial research use under a Harvard University license (see the repo's LICENSE) — not a standard permissive open-source license, so commercial use requires separate arrangement.

tutorial_intro:
Keypoint-MoSeq takes keypoint tracking data you've already generated with a separate pose-estimation tool — it doesn't do the initial tracking itself. It loads directly from several common formats (SLEAP, DeepLabCut, anipose, sleap-anipose, or NWB). A GPU is strongly recommended given its JAX backend; see the docs for exact installation and CUDA setup, which is OS/driver-dependent.

tutorial_steps:
Load your keypoints with <code>kpms.load_keypoints(keypoint_data_path, format)</code>, where format is one of <code>'sleap'</code>, <code>'deeplabcut'</code>, <code>'anipose'</code>, <code>'sleap-anipose'</code>, or <code>'nwb'</code>.
Configure two key hyperparameters in the project config: <code>latent_dimension</code> (a good heuristic is the number of PCs needed to explain 90% of variance, or 10 — whichever is lower) and <code>kappa</code> (the "stickiness" hyperparameter that sets the target syllable timescale — higher values give longer, coarser syllables; lower values give shorter, finer-grained ones).
Fitting happens in two phases: first an AR-HMM is fit to a fixed pose trajectory derived from PCA of egocentrically-aligned keypoints (this is what the paper calls "MoSeq applied to keypoints," useful as a diagnostic baseline), then the full keypoint-MoSeq model is initialized from that AR-HMM and fit via Gibbs sampling — around 500 iterations, which took 30-60 minutes on a consumer GPU for about 5 hours of data in the paper (compute time scales roughly linearly with dataset size).
Because fitting is stochastic, results differ slightly across random seeds. Fit several models (the paper used ~20) and rank them with the "expected marginal likelihood" score described in the paper to select — or build a consensus from — the best-fitting run, rather than trusting a single fit.
For downstream analysis, the package's built-in analysis pipeline covers syllable transition graphs, grid movies of each syllable, and group comparisons (for example a Kruskal-Wallis test for syllables that differ significantly in frequency between conditions) — see the "analysis" section of the docs linked above.

tutorial_output:
Per-frame syllable labels (the discrete behavioral state active at each timepoint, typically ~25-50 distinct syllables for a standard open-field session), a denoised/inferred pose trajectory that's robust to keypoint jitter and brief occlusions, and model diagnostics (the fitted transition matrix and per-syllable duration distribution) — each syllable corresponds to a recognizable, sub-second-to-second movement motif such as a rear, turn, or grooming bout.
