summary:
A reusable behavioral analysis pipeline combining DeepLabCut (pose estimation), Keypoint-MoSeq (unsupervised syllable decomposition), and DeepOF (supervised trait extraction) to characterize conditioned behavioral responses in mice — the pipeline quantifies syllable abundance changes (Δ vectors), transition dynamics (Manhattan distance on transition matrices), Shannon entropy, and supervised trait profiles (radar plots with IoU), and reveals age-dependent deficits in second-order conditioning that traditional freezing measures miss entirely.

abstract_long:
The paper introduces a data-driven behavioral analysis pipeline applied to a light-tone second-order conditioning (SOC) paradigm in mice, but the pipeline itself is the reusable contribution: it chains DeepLabCut for markerless pose estimation (14 body points, ResNet-50 backbone, trained on 20 labeled frames per video), Keypoint-MoSeq for unsupervised decomposition of tracking data into behavioral syllables (9 latent dimensions, kappa 1e10/1e4, 90% variance explained), and DeepOF for extracting supervised behavioral traits (huddling, sniffing, climbing, speed, looking around) into radar plots. The analysis has two complementary arms: unsupervised (syllable abundance Δ vectors tested with Hotelling's T² and syllable-level ANOVAs; transition matrix dynamics quantified by Manhattan distance with permutation tests; Shannon entropy of normalized transition matrices) and supervised (trait profiles visualized as radar plots, changes quantified as Intersection over Union with permutation tests). The pipeline revealed that CS1 (first-order) and CS2 (second-order) elicits qualitatively distinct behavioral repertoires — not just weaker versions of each other — and detected age-related impairments in SOC responses that were completely invisible to traditional freezing/huddling measures.

tutorial_intro:
The pipeline at <a href="https://github.com/marccanela/BehavioralProfiles">github.com/marccanela/BehavioralProfiles</a> takes DeepLabCut tracking output (h5/csv files of keypoint coordinates) as its starting point and runs the full downstream analysis: Keypoint-MoSeq syllable extraction, Δ vector computation, transition matrix analysis, Shannon entropy, and DeepOF trait extraction into radar plots. DeepLabCut itself must be run separately first to generate the pose files. The analysis is split into two complementary arms — unsupervised (syllable-based) and supervised (trait-based) — and all statistical tests used in the paper (Hotelling's T², permutation tests, Wilcoxon, ANOVA, paired t-tests) are implemented in the pipeline scripts.

tutorial_steps:
Run <a href="https://github.com/DeepLabCut/DeepLabCut">DeepLabCut</a> on your videos to generate keypoint tracking files (h5 or csv). The paper used DLC 3.0 with a ResNet-50 backbone, 14 body points, 20 labeled frames per video. Exclude tail keypoints if tracking reliability is low.
Feed the DLC output into <a href="https://github.com/dattalab/keypoint-moseq">Keypoint-MoSeq</a> to extract behavioral syllables. Key hyperparameters: 9 latent dimensions (set to explain ~90% of variance), kappa values 1e10 and 1e4. The output is a per-frame syllable label sequence for each animal.
Using the BehavioralProfiles scripts, compute syllable abundance: divide each session into 1-min bins immediately before and after cue onset, count frames per syllable per bin, then compute Δ vectors (post minus pre) per animal. Test whether the multivariate Δ distribution differs from zero using Hotelling's T² (via <code>scipy</code> or the provided helper); test individual syllable differences with one-way ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis depending on Shapiro-Wilk normality, with Tukey or Wilcoxon post hoc.
Compute transition dynamics: build unidirectional transition matrices (self-transitions removed) per animal per time bin, normalize by row-wise conditional probabilities, and calculate Manhattan distance (MD) between pre- and post-stimulus matrices per animal. Assess significance with permutation tests (shuffle time labels). Compute Shannon entropy of the normalized matrices to measure behavioral stereotypy — decrease in entropy = more constrained, repetitive behavior.
To assess whether two conditions elicit qualitatively distinct or merely scaled responses, compute within-subject Pearson correlations between syllable Δ vectors across conditions and test against a null of perfect correlation (r=1) using a one-sample t-test. For a higher-dimensional view, apply MDS with Mahalanobis distance to the syllable usage data, compute centroids per behavioral state, and calculate the angle α between the CS1 and CS2 vectors from baseline — as dimensionality increases, growing α indicates qualitatively distinct trajectories in behavioral space.
For the supervised arm, run <a href="https://github.com/mlfpm/deepof">DeepOF</a> on the DLC tracking files to extract predefined behavioral traits (huddling, sniffing, climbing, speed, looking around). Average trait values within time bins (0–1 range) and visualize as radar plots. Quantify behavioral profile changes using Intersection over Union (IoU) between pre- and post-stimulus profiles and test significance with permutation tests.

tutorial_output:
Per-animal Δ vectors (syllables x conditions matrix); Hotelling's T² p-values and syllable-level ANOVA results; Manhattan distance values and permutation p-values per condition; Shannon entropy time series; within-subject Pearson correlation coefficients between conditions; MDS angle α across dimensions; DeepOF trait time series; radar plots with IoU values and permutation p-values — all organized per experimental group, enabling direct comparison of unsupervised and supervised behavioral signatures.
