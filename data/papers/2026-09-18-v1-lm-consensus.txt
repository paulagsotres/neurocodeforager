summary:
Using iLQR-VAE — a control-based variational method that jointly infers latent circuit dynamics and unobserved trial-by-trial external inputs — the authors fit biologically constrained excitation-inhibition circuit models to simultaneous V1-LM recordings and optogenetic perturbations, finding that reciprocal long-range excitatory connections between the two areas create an approximate line attractor that dynamically builds consensus between them about both the presence and identity of a visual stimulus.

abstract_long:
The authors built latent circuit models of joint V1-LM dynamics constrained to reflect known cortical circuit organization: separate excitatory/inhibitory subpopulations within each area (Dale's law), with only excitatory units projecting across areas, and neurons assigned a definite E or I identity via a structured sparsity penalty. Trial-by-trial external inputs (visual stimulus and optogenetic perturbation) were inferred per trial using iLQR-VAE, a control-based sequential variational autoencoder that uses the iterative linear-quadratic-regulator algorithm as its recognition model. Fitted models generalized across animals, correctly labeled directly photo-stimulated PV+ interneurons as inhibitory in most fits, and predicted the effect of held-out optogenetic perturbations on unperturbed neurons — validation that the constrained circuit structure reflects real biology rather than an arbitrary parameterization. The fitted dynamics revealed that sustained visual responses depend on long-range (not local) recurrent connections, and that these reciprocal excitatory connections between V1 and LM create an approximate "line attractor": activity patterns where the two areas agree decay slowly, while patterns where they disagree decay quickly. A minimal, hand-derived E-I circuit model reproduced this mechanism analytically and showed it extends naturally to stimulus-identity consensus (not just stimulus-presence consensus) when connections carry feature-specific structure. Predictions of the model — that "agreeing" activity patterns should have longer autocorrelation timescales than "disagreeing" ones, and that silencing one area should speed up fluctuations in the other — were confirmed directly in the neural data, independent of the fitted model itself.

tutorial_intro:
iLQR-VAE is a general-purpose method for learning input-driven latent dynamics from neural population recordings — this paper's specific circuit constraints (E-I structure, area partitioning, cross-area excitation-only connections) are a particular application of it, implemented on top of the base iLQR-VAE package rather than shipped as a separate turnkey tool.

tutorial_steps:
Install iLQR-VAE from the linked repository (a JAX-based Python package) and review the base method's own examples before adapting it to a multi-area or circuit-constrained setting like this paper's.
Prepare your data as binned spike counts (this paper used 5 ms bins) per neuron per trial, along with any known trial-structured external events you want the model to condition on (for example, stimulus onset/offset times, or optogenetic perturbation windows) — these inform the time-varying prior variance placed on each input channel, per equation (6) of the paper, rather than being fed to the model as hard-coded inputs.
Define your latent circuit's structure: the paper partitions latent units into areas and, within each area, into excitatory/inhibitory subpopulations, constraining the recurrent weight matrix so only excitatory units project across areas (their equation 10) — adapt this block structure to whatever circuit hypothesis you want to test, or omit it entirely for an unconstrained baseline model to check whether your constraints hurt model fit.
If you want neurons to be assigned an unambiguous cell-type identity (as this paper does for excitatory vs. inhibitory), add a structured sparsity penalty on the readout matrix (their equation 12) that discourages a neuron from loading onto both an excitatory and inhibitory latent subpopulation simultaneously.
Fit the model by maximizing the evidence lower bound (ELBO) via iLQR-VAE's control-based recognition model, which solves for the most likely trial-specific input trajectory using the iterative LQR algorithm rather than a separate encoder network.
Select hyperparameters (number of latent units, number of input channels) via cross-validation on held-out trials, and validate the fitted model with domain-specific checks where available — this paper used the known identity of directly photo-stimulated PV+ interneurons and the model's ability to predict held-out optogenetic perturbation effects as two such checks; reject or refit models that fail these checks.
To probe the resulting circuit's dynamics (for example, testing for a line attractor or characterizing relaxation timescales), linearize the fitted nonlinear dynamics around a period of interest and examine the eigenvalues/eigenvectors of the resulting linear system, or systematically down-scale specific connectivity blocks (for example, long-range vs. local) and measure the resulting change in the network's response.

tutorial_output:
A fitted latent circuit model per animal, with inferred trial-specific external input trajectories, a decoded excitatory/inhibitory identity per recorded neuron, and — from downstream analysis of the fitted dynamics — relaxation time constants, eigenmode decompositions, and predicted responses to novel (including held-out or simulated) perturbations, usable to test specific circuit-mechanistic hypotheses about how the recorded areas interact.
