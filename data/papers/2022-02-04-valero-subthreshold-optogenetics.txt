summary:
Valero et al. use brief optogenetic depolarizing pulses delivered via μLED probes to probe the subthreshold excitability of large CA1 pyramidal neuron populations in freely moving mice, without disrupting their normal spiking. During SPW-Rs excitability decreases (balanced E/I model), during theta and within place fields it increases (reciprocal model); critically, optogenetic unmasking reveals stable latent place fields in 69% of apparent non-place cells, implying the fraction of CA1 neurons with a place representation is far higher than classical firing-threshold criteria suggest.

abstract_long:
The paper develops a population-scale, non-invasive proxy for membrane potential dynamics by delivering brief (20 ms), low-power μLED pulses to CA1 pyramidal neurons at random intervals while monitoring their spike responses. The magnitude of the evoked firing-rate change (ΔRate = μLED response − control) serves as a readout of the neuron's instantaneous excitability (Vm). Three main findings emerge. First, during SPW-Rs individual pyramidal neurons decrease their light-evoked responses (negative gain, balanced E/I model), even though population firing increases — resolving an apparent paradox between population and single-cell excitability. Second, during theta oscillations and within place fields, light-evoked responses are enhanced (reciprocal/disinhibition model), consistent with place cells receiving reduced inhibition inside their fields. Third — the most striking result — optogenetic depolarization unmasks stable, spatially selective "ghost" place fields in 69% of neurons classified as non-place cells under normal conditions; these latent fields are spatially correlated with the sparse spontaneous spikes of the same cells, are as stable as real place fields, and their SPW-R cofiring patterns predict their spatial overlap on the track, just as for real place cells. The implication is that essentially the entire CA1 pyramidal population carries a spatial representation, with most cells held below firing threshold by inhibition under normal conditions.

tutorial_intro:
The analysis code lives in <a href="https://github.com/buzsakilab/buzcode">buzcode</a> and <a href="https://github.com/valegarman/HippoCookBook">HippoCookBook</a> (MATLAB). The key analysis concept is ΔRate: for each neuron, subtract the firing rate during matched no-light control windows from the firing rate during light-pulse windows. This single number — computed per brain state, per theta phase bin, or per spatial position bin — is the excitability proxy that drives all the paper's analyses. The required input is a standard buzcode/HippoCookBook session struct with spike times, light pulse times (stored as events), LFP, and animal position.

tutorial_steps:
Classify light-responsive units using <code>getOptogeneticResponse.m</code> (HippoCookBook): it computes the cross-correlogram between each unit's spikes and the μLED pulse times, then applies four statistical tests (mean ΔRate > 2 SD, P < 10⁻³ vs shuffled pulses, bootstrap, SALT latency jitter). Returns a per-unit <code>optogeneticResponse</code> struct with <code>.isResponsive</code>, <code>.deltaRate</code> per intensity level, and response PSTHs. Only responsive units are used as the excitability proxy.
Compute ΔRate across brain states with <code>getAveragedPeriEventResponses.m</code>: pass spike times, light pulse event times, and a brain state vector (from <code>SleepScoreMaster.m</code>) to get mean ΔRate separately for Run, Quiet, NREM, and REM. For SPW-R-aligned analysis, pass SPW-R peak times (from <code>rippleMasterDetector.m</code>) as the reference event to get ΔRate as a function of time relative to ripple onset — this produces the "negative gain during SPW-R" result.
Compute ΔRate as a function of spatial position: bin the animal's track position into 2.2-cm bins and compute mean ΔRate per bin per neuron using <code>getSpikesReturnPlaceFields.m</code>. Comparing in-field vs out-of-field ΔRate (using the place field boundaries from control epochs) gives the within-field excitability gain. The same function run on light-stimulation epochs for non-place cells identifies unmasked (ghost) fields as spatial bins with significantly elevated ΔRate that are stable across trial halves.
For population assembly analysis, run <code>getAssemblies.m</code> (buzcode) on the z-scored spike matrix of all pyramidal neurons during home-cage SPW-Rs to extract ICA assembly membership weights. Then correlate assembly membership with pairwise spatial overlap on the track using <code>getSpikesReturnPlaceFields.m</code> output. To test replay, run <code>replayDetect.m</code> (buzcode) with and without unmasked place field spikes included in the place field sequence template, and compare the fraction of SPW-R events with significant Bayesian trajectory probabilities.

tutorial_output:
Per-unit <code>optogeneticResponse</code> struct (ΔRate per intensity, brain state, and spatial bin); place field maps for control and light epochs with ghost field flags; ICA assembly weight matrix; pairwise spatial-vs-ripple cofiring correlation matrices; replay trajectory significance fractions with and without unmasked fields included.
