About

I'm Paula Gomez Sotres, a postdoctoral researcher studying social behavior and neural computation. Throughout my work, I've noticed a consistent problem: labs spend countless hours reinventing analysis pipelines that already exist, scattered across GitHub repositories and supplementary materials.

That's why I created neurocodeforager. It's a searchable index of open-source tools cited in neuroscience papers — pulled from code availability statements, categorized, and paired with short usage guides. The goal is simple: help labs standardize methods instead of reinventing analyses from scratch.

What I do

My research focuses on understanding how neural circuits encode complex social information. I'm interested in the intersection of behavior and computation — how animals process social cues, and what principles the brain uses to do it.

Outside the lab, I'm passionate about open science and making research tools more accessible. NeuroCodeForager is a side project that combines my interests in coding and knowledge transmission.

The problem NeuroCodeForager solves

  • Many neuroscience papers mention code but bury the link in supplementary materials or acknowledgments.
  • There's no central place to find tools by method (calcium imaging, pose estimation, electrophysiology, etc.).
  • Labs don't always know that a tool for their problem already exists — so they build it again.

NeuroCodeForager changes that. It extracts, organizes, and makes searchable the tools that already exist. Relaying on AI tools to help classification, we take papers and classify them to find useful tools.
